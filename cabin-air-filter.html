Cabin air filter

A filter that cleans the outside air before your heater and air conditioning blow it into the passenger area.

In plain English

The cabin air filter is a pleated paper or fabric pad that sits in the air duct feeding your car's heating and air conditioning system. As outside air is pulled in, the filter catches dust, pollen, soot, and other particles so less of it reaches you; some versions include charcoal to soak up odors. It is usually tucked behind the glove box or under the hood near the bottom of the windshield. It is a different part from the engine air filter, which cleans the air the engine breathes.

Why it's on your estimate

A shop recommends a new one when the filter looks dirty or clogged during an inspection or it is due according to the maintenance schedule. A clogged filter can cause weak airflow from the vents, a fan that sounds louder or strained, musty or outside smells in the car, more dust on the dashboard, and windows that fog up and take longer to clear. Cars driven in dusty areas, heavy traffic, or wildfire smoke may need it changed more often.

How urgent is it?

Can usually wait

A dirty cabin air filter does not affect how the car drives or brakes, so it can usually be handled at your next regular service; check the owner's manual for how often it should be changed. It matters more if the windows are slow to defog in cold or wet weather, if someone in the car has allergies or breathing problems, or if the vent fan is straining to push air through it.

Questions to ask the shop

  • Can you show me the old filter so I can see how dirty it is?
  • Is this the cabin air filter for the vents or the engine air filter, and do both need replacing?
  • Where is the filter on my car, and is it something I could check myself next time?

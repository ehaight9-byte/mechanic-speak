Mechanic Speak

Ball joint

A pivot that connects the wheel to the suspension so it can steer and move up and down over bumps.

In plain English

A ball joint is a metal ball on a stud that sits in a greased socket, a bit like a hip joint. It connects the control arm, a hinged arm of the suspension, to the steering knuckle, the part the wheel is mounted on. That pivot lets the wheel turn left and right for steering while it also moves up and down over bumps. A rubber cover called a boot keeps the grease in and dirt and water out.

Why it's on your estimate

A shop recommends a new ball joint when it finds more looseness in the joint than the car maker allows, or a torn boot that has let the grease out and dirt in. The usual signs are clunking or creaking over bumps or when turning, steering that feels loose or wanders, and front tires wearing unevenly on one edge. Other worn steering and suspension parts cause the same symptoms, so the looseness should be traced to the joint itself.

How urgent is it?

Don't wait

Ball joints wear gradually, but one that is loose beyond the car maker's limit is a safety problem, because a joint that comes apart lets the wheel fold in or out and you can lose control of the car. It is more serious still if the clunking is getting louder or the steering feels loose, so have it checked before driving much further, especially at highway speeds.

Questions to ask the shop

  • Which ball joint is worn (upper or lower, left or right), and can you show me the looseness?
  • How much play did you measure compared with the car maker's limit?
  • Does the joint come out on its own or with the whole control arm, and will the car need a wheel alignment afterward?

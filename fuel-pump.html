Mechanic Speak

Fuel pump

An electric pump, usually inside the gas tank, that pushes fuel up to the engine.

In plain English

The engine needs a steady supply of fuel at the right pressure to run. The fuel pump is a small electric motor that pushes gasoline from the tank through a line to the fuel injectors, the nozzles that spray fuel into the engine. On most modern cars the pump sits inside the fuel tank, often built into one assembly with the sender that works your fuel gauge. The fuel around it helps keep the pump cool.

Why it's on your estimate

A shop recommends a new fuel pump when testing shows it can no longer deliver enough fuel or pressure, or when it has stopped running altogether. Typical signs are an engine that cranks but will not start, sputtering or stalling, weak acceleration, or losing power going uphill or carrying a load. A blown fuse, a bad relay, poor wiring, or a clogged fuel filter can cause the same symptoms, so the diagnosis matters.

How urgent is it?

Fix soon

A weak pump can keep working for a while, but it tends to get worse and can quit without much warning, leaving you with a car that will not start. It becomes more serious if the engine is already stalling or cutting out while you drive, because losing power in traffic is a safety risk.

Questions to ask the shop

  • Did you measure the fuel pressure, and what was the reading compared with what it should be?
  • Did you check the fuse, relay, and wiring to the pump before deciding the pump itself is bad?
  • Is the fuel filter being replaced or checked as part of this job?

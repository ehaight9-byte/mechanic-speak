Mechanic Speak

Serpentine belt

One long rubber belt that the engine spins to power things like the alternator and A/C.

In plain English

The serpentine belt is one long rubber belt that snakes around several pulleys at the front of the engine. As the engine turns, the belt spins the parts that keep the car running comfortably: the alternator that charges the battery, the air conditioning compressor, and on many cars the power steering pump and the water pump.

Why it's on your estimate

Rubber belts crack, fray, and stretch with age and miles. A shop will usually recommend a new one when they see cracks or glazing, hear squealing, or when the car reaches the mileage the maker lists for replacement.

How urgent is it?

Fix soon

A worn belt often keeps working for a while, but if it snaps the battery stops charging and, on many cars, the engine can overheat quickly. Don't put it off for long once it's visibly cracked.

Questions to ask the shop

  • Can you show me the cracks or wear on the belt?
  • Does the tensioner or any pulley need replacing too, or just the belt?

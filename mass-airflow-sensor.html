Mechanic Speak

Mass airflow sensor

A sensor that measures how much air enters the engine so the car's computer can add the right amount of fuel.

In plain English

The mass airflow sensor, often written as MAF sensor, sits in the air intake tube between the air filter and the engine. It uses a tiny heated wire or film: the more air rushes past, the more power it takes to keep that wire hot, and the sensor turns that into a reading. The engine computer uses the reading to decide how much fuel to spray in, so the mix of air and fuel stays balanced. If the sensor reports the wrong amount of air, the engine gets too much fuel (called running rich) or too little (called running lean).

Why it's on your estimate

A shop recommends cleaning or replacing the sensor when the check engine light comes on with an airflow-sensor code or tests show its readings are off, often because dirt or oil has coated the sensing wire. Common symptoms are a rough or unsteady idle, hesitation or jerking when you accelerate, stalling, hard starting, worse fuel economy, and sometimes black smoke from the tailpipe. An air leak in the intake hose or a poorly fitted air filter can cause the same code, so those should be ruled out first.

How urgent is it?

Fix soon

A car with a faulty airflow sensor can often still be driven gently for a short time, but it may run poorly, waste fuel, or switch into a reduced-power mode to protect itself. It becomes more serious if the engine stalls in traffic, hesitates badly when you pull out, or the check engine light flashes, and running too rich or too lean for a long time can damage the engine and the catalytic converter.

Questions to ask the shop

  • What trouble code or test result points to the airflow sensor, and did you check for air leaks or a bad air filter first?
  • Could cleaning the sensor fix the problem, or does it need to be replaced?
  • If you replace it, how will we confirm the problem is fixed, such as a test drive and clearing the codes?

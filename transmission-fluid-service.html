Transmission fluid service

Draining the old fluid from the transmission and refilling it with fresh fluid, sometimes with a new filter.

In plain English

The transmission is the gearbox that transfers the engine's power to the wheels, and it runs in a bath of special oil called transmission fluid. The fluid lubricates the moving parts, carries heat away, and in an automatic it also works as hydraulic fluid — the pressurized liquid that actually makes the gears shift. Over time, heat and friction break the fluid down and fill it with tiny metal particles, so it stops protecting as well. A transmission fluid service drains out the old fluid and refills it with fresh fluid that meets your car's specification; on many cars the shop also replaces or cleans the transmission filter.

Why it's on your estimate

Shops recommend it as preventive maintenance because dirty, worn-out fluid makes shifting rough and accelerates wear inside the transmission — and a transmission rebuild is one of the most expensive repairs a car can need. Signs the fluid is overdue include hard or delayed shifting, slipping (the engine revs but the car doesn't pull), or a burnt smell from the fluid. Follow the interval in your owner's manual, since it varies widely between cars.

How urgent is it?

Fix soon

This is maintenance, not an emergency — driving a bit past the service point won't destroy anything overnight. But the longer worn fluid stays in, the more wear accumulates, and once shifting problems start, a simple fluid service may no longer be enough.

Questions to ask the shop

  • What does my owner's manual say the service interval is for my car?
  • Is this a drain-and-fill or a full flush, and does my car need the filter replaced too?
  • Will you use the exact fluid specification my manufacturer requires?

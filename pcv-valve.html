Mechanic Speak

PCV valve

A small one-way valve that routes engine fumes back into the engine to be burned instead of leaking out.

In plain English

PCV stands for positive crankcase ventilation. As your engine runs, some combustion gases slip past the pistons into the crankcase — the lower part of the engine that holds the oil. These gases are called blow-by, and the PCV valve is a small one-way valve that sends them back through the intake manifold (the passage that feeds air into the engine) so they get burned up instead of building pressure or polluting. It also keeps pressure from building up inside the crankcase, which protects the engine's gaskets and seals.

Why it's on your estimate

Shops recommend replacing it when it's clogged with sludge or carbon, or when symptoms point at it — rough idle, a hissing or whistling sound from the engine, oil leaks around gaskets, or the check engine light. A stuck-open valve acts like a vacuum leak and can cause misfires and oil burning; a stuck-closed one traps pressure that forces oil past seals.

How urgent is it?

Fix soon

A bad PCV valve rarely strands you the same day, but it gets worse the longer it goes — trapped pressure can ruin seals and gaskets, and the sludge it allows can shorten engine life. If you notice fresh oil leaks or the engine running rough, get it checked promptly.

Questions to ask the shop

  • What makes you think the PCV valve is the problem — did you test it, or are you going by symptoms?
  • Should the PCV hose be replaced at the same time, or just the valve?
  • Could low oil or overdue oil changes have caused this to clog?

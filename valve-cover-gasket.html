Mechanic Speak

Valve cover gasket

A seal around the lid on top of the engine that keeps the oil inside from leaking out.

In plain English

The top of the engine holds moving parts called valves, which are constantly splashed with oil to keep them lubricated. A lid called the valve cover is bolted over them, and the valve cover gasket is the seal, usually molded rubber, squeezed between that lid and the engine. Its job is to keep the oil in and dirt out. Years of engine heat make the gasket hard and brittle, and then it starts to let oil through.

Why it's on your estimate

A shop recommends replacing it when oil is leaking from the joint around the valve cover. The usual signs are oily, dirt-caked grime around the top of the engine, a burning oil smell when leaked oil lands on hot parts, and an oil level that drops between oil changes. On some engines the oil seeps into the wells where the spark plugs sit and makes the engine run rough or misfire.

How urgent is it?

Fix soon

A light seep is usually not an emergency as long as you keep an eye on the oil level and top it up when needed, but these leaks tend to grow rather than stop. It becomes more serious if oil is dripping onto the hot exhaust, the engine is misfiring, or the oil level keeps dropping, because leaking oil can smoke or catch fire and an engine run low on oil can be badly damaged.

Questions to ask the shop

  • Can you show me where the oil is coming from, and is it a light seep or an active drip?
  • How did you confirm it is the valve cover gasket and not another oil leak nearby?
  • Has oil reached the spark plugs or ignition coils, and do any of those need attention too?
